The 2000 ACM South Africa Programming Contest

Sponsored by IBM

Hosted by the University of Pretoria

Additional locations (to be confirmed) at Rhodes University and the University of Cape Town

Date of Contest: Saturday, 16 September 2000

Early registration deadline: 31 August 2000

 

Teams from all over Southern Africa are invited to register for, and participate in, the second South African regional contest. The region includes: Angola, Botswana, Lesotho, Madagascar, Mozambique, Namibia, South Africa, Swaziland, Zambia, Zimbabwe; furthermore, we welcome any other African teams who are unable to join the Northern African regional contest.

To keep up to date with the planning, please subscribe to this web page or visit it regularly. The official web pages of the International Contest are at http://acm.baylor.edu/acmicpc/. There, you will find links to:

  • The rules, including: your team's composition, the structure of the contest, conduct at the contest, programming languages used, and mandatory attendance at some of the contest-day functions.
  • The registration system, which will allow your team coach to register for the contest and obtain updated information. In addition to registering through the system, please email the Regional Contest Director as well.

You must register before 31 August 2000. IBM is providing copies of the software (the compilers and development environments which will be used at the contest) to all registered teams, and the sooner you register, the sooner you can start practicing and becoming accustomed to the integrated development environment and compilers. The following sections provide some more information about local arrangements. If you need additional information, please contact the Regional Contest Director.

Locations for Southern Africa

The contest will be held simultaneously at the University of Pretoria and (provisionally) Rhodes University (in Grahamstown, South Africa) and at the University of Cape Town. (At all three locations, the Departments of Computer Science are the hosts.) When you register, please inform us of your preferred location. Because of the widely varying economic situations in the region, no contest fees will be charged. The teams are, however, responsible for their own transportation and accommodation costs. At a minimum, teams should plan to stay near the contest location the night before the contest, since the contest will get underway first thing in the morning (precise times will be announced later).

Localization of the contest rules

The reference rules are available on the ACM's contest web site. All team members must bring valid student identification and coaches must be prepared to vouch for the team members' student status. The following are region-specific localizations of the rules:

  1. We will take a baccalaureate degree to be a four-year Honours degree. As such, the Honours year does not count as post-graduate study (a graduate degree, in American terminology), but rather as part of your bachelor's degree (undergraduate).
  2. The contest will last five (5) hours (not counting additional activities, which will be mandatory to qualify).
  3. There will be at least six (6) problems to be solved.
  4. Multiple teams may be entered by any given university or technikon. In the event that a location is oversubscribed (full), universities may be asked to prioritize their teams.
  5. Languages to be used will be C, C++, Java and Delphi.
