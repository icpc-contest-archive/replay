Contest Rules 
 Team Composition 


 ** A representative of the sponsoring Austrealina institution of higher 
    education, typically a faculty member, must serve as the team coach. 
    The coach certifies the eligibility of contestants and serves as the 
    point-of-contact with the team prior to and during contest activities. 
    A team may only have one coach. 
    Each team that consists of three contestants and satisfies the following 
    eligibility rules:

     1. contestants are students enrolled in a degree program at the sponsoring 
	institution with at least a half-time load. This rule is not to 
	be construed as disqualifying co-op students, exchange students, 
        or students serving internships. 
     2. A student must not have completed her/his High School more than five 
        years ago.  
     3. team has been enrolled through the official ICPC web site
        http://icpc.baylor.edu/regionals/finder/aupc-2013

    is qualified for winning the AUPC championship or one of the
    prizes.  A student who FIRST began post-secondary studies in 2008 or earlier 
    is not eligible to being a member of a qualified team.  

 ** Frequently asked Questions:
     Q: can a student change teams for the South Pacific Programming Contest?
     A: Yes. AUPC and SPPC are different contests.

     Q: can teams from outside Australia participate?
     A: yes. Teams can register in http://icpc.baylor.edu/regionals/finder/aupc-2013
        under the "Guests" site and participate in any of the above mentioned
        supervised locations.

     Q: can teams with students not satisfying one or more of the above conditions
        participate in AUPC?
     A: yes.  But they will not qualify for the championship or one of the prizes.

     Q: can more than one team from the same institution win a cash prize?
     A: No, unless we run out of eligible teams from other institutions.

     more will be added, if necessary.

Scoring of the AUPC Contest


    A problem is solved when it is accepted by the judges. The judges are 
    solely responsible for accepting or rejecting submitted runs. 
    The Contest Organizers are empowered to adjust for or adjudicate 
    unforeseen events and conditions. Their decisions are final. 

    The total time for a team is the sum of the time consumed 
    for each problem solved.  The time consumed for a solved problem is the 
    time elapsed from the beginning of the contest to the submittal of the 
    accepted run plus 20 penalty minutes for every rejected run for that 
    problem regardless of submittal time. 
    There is no time added for a problem that is not solved. 
  
    Teams are ranked according to the most problems solved.
    Teams who solve the same number of problems are ranked by least total time. 
  

AUPC Contest Environment/Conduct


    DOMJudge will be used as the system for judging and display of scoreboard.
       Never used it before? do not you worry, you only need a browser to view
       a demo in http://domjudge.sourceforge.net/demo.
       Press the "documentation" button and read the "team manual".  It is not
       long and has a lot of pictures.

    The programming languages of the AUPC only include Java, C, and C++. 
    Submissions in Python/C# are allowed and will be judged for teams with no interest
    prizes or the AUPC championship. 
            A team that submits a solution in Python/C#, regardless of whether it
            has been accepted or rejected, will be automatically disqualified from 
            winning the AUPC championship or any of the prizes. 
            But the team's rank on the contest scoreboard will NOT be affected.  
            You have been warned!

    A team's submission will be compiled, run on judge's data with a judgement
    communicated back to the team.  Judge's decision is one of those listed 
    in  http://www.cse.unsw.edu.au/~elgindyh/AuPC/messages.pdf 
  
    Each team will use a single workstation provided by the contest organizer. 
    No other electronic devices or storage media are permitted.
    A single hardcopy of the problems will be made available for each team.
    Contestants are not allowed to touch their allocated computer, nor the problem
    set, until the start of the contest.

    Contestants may bring ONLY printed materials for use during the contest. 
  
    At least six problems will be posed. So far as possible, problems will avoid 
    dependence on detailed knowledge of a particular application area or particular 
    contest language.  
  
    The contest runs for 5 consecutive hours.
