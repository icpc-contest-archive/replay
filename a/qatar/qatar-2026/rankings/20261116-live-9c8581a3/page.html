    Qatar Collegiate Programming Contest Brings Together 135 Student Teams

    16 October 2026Updated:16 October 20262 Mins Read
    Copy Link WhatsApp Twitter Facebook Email LinkedIn
    Follow Us
    Instagram Facebook X (Twitter) TikTok Snapchat LinkedIn WhatsApp
    Share
    Facebook Twitter LinkedIn WhatsApp Email Copy Link

    Carnegie Mellon University in Qatar (CMU-Q) hosted the seventh annual Qatar Collegiate Programming Contest (QCPC), attracting 100 high school teams and 35 university teams for a competition focused on coding, teamwork and problem-solving.

    Organised by the Hamad Bin Jassim Center for Computer Science Education (HBJ Center), the contest is considered the largest national programming competition in the GCC region. The HBJ Center is a collaboration between CMU-Q and the Jassim and Hamad Bin Jassim Charitable Foundation, while prizes were sponsored by Doha Insurance Group.

    The two-day competition challenged participants to solve a series of programming problems under strict time constraints, testing their technical skills, creativity and ability to work effectively as a team.

    Michael Trick, Dean of CMU-Q, said, ‘The Qatar Collegiate Programming Contest has helped create a community of young programmers in Qatar. As young programmers develop their skills, this community flourishes and creates a culture of innovation, progress and creativity in Qatar.’

    Ali Ibrahim Al Gharib, Chief Executive Officer of the Jassim and Hamad Bin Jassim Charitable Foundation, said, ‘We are proud to see the reach of the Qatar Collegiate Programming Contest continue to grow and attract such creative young minds. Equipping students with advanced computing skills is essential for driving innovation.’

    In the university category, teams from Carnegie Mellon University in Qatar secured the gold, silver and bronze medals.

    In the high school category, DPS-Modern Indian School won gold, Park House English School claimed silver, and Birla Public School took bronze.

    The top two teams from both the university and high school divisions will advance to the Africa and Arab Collegiate Programming Championship (ACPC).

    Eduardo Feo Flushing, Assistant Teaching Professor of Computer Science and lead organiser of the competition, said the standard of problem-solving demonstrated by participants was particularly strong this year.

    Since its launch, QCPC has provided students with opportunities to develop technical skills, collaborate with peers and gain experience in competitive programming. The competition continues to support the development of computing talent in Qatar and the wider region.

    For more information about Carnegie Mellon University in Qatar (CMU-Q) visit their website: qatar.cmu.edu
