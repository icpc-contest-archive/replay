...announcing the North Central North America Regionals of the 26th Annual

ACM International Collegiate Programming Contest

sponsored by IBM

Saturday, November 10, 2001 at a site near you!

The Contest

 Each fall, every ACM region worldwide holds a regional programming contest. Each contest provides the mechanism for selecting the top teams in the region, and for identifying those teams that will advance to the annual World Finals of The ACM International Collegiate Programming Contest. This year the World Finals will be held March 20-24, 2002 in Honolulu, Hawaii.  The contest is again sponsored by IBM.

The Region

 The ACM North Central North America (NCNA) region includes Minnesota, Wisconsin, Western Ontario, Manitoba, Iowa, North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, and Kansas. Schools from neighboring states or provinces may choose to participate in the NCNA region with approval of the directors of each region involved.  As it is difficult for some teams to travel to a single central site, the contest is conducted concurrently at multiple sites hosted by volunteer schools across the region. The regional contest will be held on Saturday, November 10, 2001.

The Contest Sites

 Last year there were eleven different contest sites making travel convenient for the participating teams.  Schools volunteering to host a contest site are given the first opportunity to have multiple teams participating in the contest.  Sites are now being selected for the 2001 contest; please contact the regional contest director if you are interested!

The Teams

 Teams consist of students in good standing at their school.  They may include three undergraduates (who have not yet received a baccalaureate degree), or two undergraduates and a graduate student.  All team members must be certified by a faculty sponsor.

The Rules

 Detailed regional rules will be posted here shortly.  Also check the ACM International Contest web page for rules that apply to all contestants and all regions.

Scoring

 Scoring is detailed in the regional rules, but basically each team will attempt to solve (by writing programs) as many of the problems presented as possible in five hours.  The team that solves the most problems wins!  Ties are broken by considering the time required to produce each solution, and the number of failed solution attempts made by the team. 

Benefits and Rewards

 The most immediate benefit of the contest is to meet teams of students from other schools and participate in a friendly competition for bragging rights to the title of "Best Team in the Region."  Additionally, sites may provide T-shirts, pizza parties, snacks, site and region-wide prizes, and an opportunity to discuss challenging employment opportunities with IBM.  Most teams, however, see the opportunity to advance to the World Finals in Honolulu, Hawaii as "the big prize."  The top few teams in the region will receive this honor, along with generous travel and housing support.  The number of teams that advance from NCNA is based on the number of teams participating, so register today!

Registration

 On-line registration is completed through the ACM International Contest web page.  The team coach must complete the registration process.  Only teams that have registered at least seven days prior to the regional contest (that is, by November 3, 2001) will be eligible to advance to the World Finals in Hawaii.  Due to limited space at each site, early registration is strongly advised.  Some sites may charge a modest registration fee, but with the generous support of IBM for the NCNA regional contest, this is not typical.

Questions?

 This page will be updated with rules, site locations, and other information in the near future.  If you have questions, please don't hesitate to e-mail the regional contest director.
