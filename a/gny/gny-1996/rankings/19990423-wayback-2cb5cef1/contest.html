ACM International Collegiate Programming Contest

Each year, the ACM hosts a programming contest open to undergraduates around the world. I have been involved with their International Collegiate Programming Contest for four years.

  • 1998-99: I trained a team for Cornell which competed in the Greater New York Regional Contest. We got first place; Columbia University got second. The contest web page at Bloomfield College is available.

    • A Cornell press release announces our achievement.
    • An article in the December 3, 1998 Cornell Chronicle.
    • A picture of me and the team is available. From the left, it's me, Mike Smullens, Erik Dangremond, and David Kempe.
    • A picture of me judging. Many thanks to Patrick Fox of Bloomfield College for the two photos.

    Green Hills Software has been kind enough to sponsor our trip to the Regional contest for the past three years.
    The team competed in the World Finals in Eindhoven, The Netherlands on Sunday, April 11. A truly exciting point came 53 minutes into the contest, when Cornell was vaulted into first place by being the first team to get 2 problems correct. We held first place until 105 minutes into the contest, when Berkeley was the first team to get 3 problems correct.

    In the end, the University of Waterloo came in first place, and Cornell was 18th of 62 teams. This is an incredible accomplishment, especially considering this is Cornell's first time at the international contest. More importantly, on a personal level at least, we just barely beat Virginia Tech, where I went as an undergrad. I was on the VT team my senior year.

    We would like to thank Green Hills Software for their generous donation which helped fund our trip to the World Finals.

  • 1997-98: I trained a team for Cornell which competed in the Greater New York Regional Contest. We got third place; SUNY Stony Brook got first, and Queens College got second. The contest web page at West Point is available.

  • 1996-97: I trained a team for Cornell which competed in the Greater New York Regional Contest. We got second place.

  • 1995-96: As a senior, I was on the Virginia Tech programming team, which won the 1995 Mid-Atlantic Regional Contest, and got 9th place at the 1996 International Contest.

Cornell Programming Competitions

Each year, I run two local programming contests at Cornell. They were sponsored by the ACSU. I also maintain the ACSU contest page. It contains the rules for the contest, as well as the problems and solutions for previous competitions.

Other Programming Contests

The best collection of programming contest links that I know of is the Programming Contest Problems Archive. This has links to ACM, IOI, USACO, and many other contests.

The AMC ICPC Problem Set Archive has 400 old ACM problems.

The University of California at Berkeley won the international contest that I participated in. Their site has their local contests from previous years, and links to other contests.

The International Obfuscated C Code Contest is not a programming contest like the previous ones listed, but it's always fun to read the winning entries.
